# Demo video research log

## 2026-11-14: What makes hackathon demo videos win (feature-heavy projects)
Full notes: [bullet_points.md](../Research/hackathon_demo_videos_market/bullet_points.md) · applied to Leadline: [application.md](../Research/hackathon_demo_videos_market/application.md)

**Limit:** no winning video could be watched (Devpost 403, Bolt/Agno pages failed, Junction Notion page needs JS). Findings come from the written rules of organizers and judges (AngelHack, Devfolio, ETHGlobal, AI Tinkerers, RevenueCat Shipaton, Chainlink) and from winners in their own words.

**Cases:** Hawkwatch (TreeHacks 2025 grand prize), ETHGlobal Bangkok 2024 finalists, GroupMe (TC Disrupt 2010, didn't win, later sold to Skype), Reworkd/AgentGPT (nwHacks 2023), Ray Deck (6/6 wins).

### Findings
1. **Length:** most limits are 2–4 min, and 3 is the most common. Hook and first proof within 60–90 s. No dead time, no speed-up.
2. **Product on screen:** the working app for about half the runtime, with 3–5 features. "A few things done really well" beats many half-done ones. Slides: at most 1–2.
3. **Opening:** one named user, one pain, in 15–30 s, then a one-line answer.
4. **Narration:** say what the user gets, not what the screen shows. Don't turn it into a feature list.
5. **Honesty:** judges ask "what was real versus mocked". Label anything mocked.
6. **Sponsor fit:** topic and rubric fit matter most. Junction partners set their own criteria. Name the sponsor tech (Cala) where it appears.
7. **Quality:** clear audio beats polish. 1080p, no phone recording, no robotic TTS, no music under text.
8. **Close:** the one-line change for the user, next steps, live link and repo.
